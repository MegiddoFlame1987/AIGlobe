Globe 1 · direction

The last 30 days as a vector

drag to rotate · big dot = selected window · small dot = last 7 days · line = how the vector moved across the window

K

Control. Do people still understand and steer the systems? −1 (drift): agents acting out of scope, deception in evals, safety teams dismantled. +1 (control): audits, published evals, kill switches, standards.

U

Use. What people do with AI. −1 (harm): attacks, fraud, deepfakes, surveillance. +1 (help): defence, patching, science, medicine.

R

Reward distribution. Who benefits. −1 (concentrated): concentration of capital and power, layoffs without a safety net. +1 (shared): open access, open standards, public science.

G

Geopolitics. The field the race runs in, states only. −1 (confrontation), by any country: export controls, chip smuggling, blocked deals on national-security grounds, military AI, state propaganda. +1 (cooperation): treaties, UN resolutions, joint institutes, dialogue between rival states.

T

Pace. How hard the race is pushing. −1 (acceleration): frontier releases, new chip generations, megadeals, data-centre build-outs. +1 (restraint): pauses, withheld releases, cancelled build-outs.

Method

How the vector is computed

Every event gets five scores, K, U, R, G and T, each from {−1, 0, +1}. Zero means the measure does not apply to that event. −1 always points to the technology side, +1 to the human side.

Axis score over the selected window (30, 180, 360 days or 5 years): (n₊ − n₋) / (n₊ + n₋). A ratio, not a sum, so more news by itself does not move the needle. Three axes from −1 to 1 form a cube; the globe shows the same point with the vector clipped to length 1.

The two sliders sit outside the globe because they measure the field, not the direction of AI itself. Geopolitics (G) uses the same ratio. Pace (T) divides by all events in the window: (n₊ − n₋) / n. Acceleration news outnumbers restraint news about ten to one, so a plain ratio would sit near −0.8 forever; the share of the whole news flow does move.

Why G and T exist: in test months from November 2022 and March 2024, about 37% of events (chips, export controls, compute build-outs) scored zero on K, U and R. With G and T that falls to 4–12%. The chip race started before ChatGPT: restrictions on advanced AI chips between the United States and China began weeks before its launch.

Neutrality

No country or bloc is the default good side or bad side: not the United States, not Europe, not China, not Russia, not anyone. Every score must survive the mirror rule: swap the actor for another country and the score must stay the same. Acts are scored, not nationalities. The sampling frame is the G20 (19 states, the EU and the African Union) plus four AI chokepoints outside it: Taiwan, the Netherlands, Israel and the UAE; not the G7, which is one bloc. No single language and no single country should supply more than 40% of events. Geopolitics applies to states only; a company or an individual is not a state. One-sided claims are attributed in the title ("X says…"). The line under the meters shows which languages the events in the window came from, so source imbalance stays visible. The full rubric is RUBRIC.md in the repository.

Sources

Once a week a single task searches the news in eight languages (EN, ZH, JA, KO, RU, ES, HI, AR), drops duplicates, scores the new events with the rubric above and appends them to data/events.json in the repository. The globe and the list show the last 180 days (data/events.json); a compact archive of every scored event (data/archive.json: date, five scores, source language, retro flag) feeds the longer windows, and daily snapshots stay forever.

Known weaknesses

  • News has a negativity bias. That is why the indicator reports a ratio and its drift, not an absolute value.
  • Statements ("CEO X says AI needs a brake") count the same as actions. This inflates the K axis.
  • History before mid-2026 is backfilled from monthly reviews (AI Incident Database, State of AI, CSET, yearly timelines). Those are curated after the fact and mostly in English, so they carry hindsight bias and a language bias; backfilled rows are flagged as retro.
  • A language model scores the events with the rubric. Agreement with human scoring has not been measured yet.
  • Language coverage is uneven: English and Chinese dominate.
  • The sign of Pace is a value judgement: it treats acceleration as a move toward technology. A reader who welcomes acceleration should read that slider the other way round.
  • The line between industrial policy (G 0) and a measure aimed at a rival bloc (G −1) is fuzzy, for example chip-fab subsidies.

Method

How the regime is computed

Each indicator is turned into a score from −1 (fear, contraction, tight money) to +1 (calm, expansion, easy money) by its percentile within the trailing five years, using only data available on that day. History on the page is what the indicator showed at the time, not a later revision.

  • Fear: VIX (Cboe, via FRED) and the Moody's Baa corporate bond yield minus the 10-year Treasury yield (via FRED). High values score as fear.
  • Growth: copper divided by gold (futures prices) and G20 breadth, the share of 19 G20 country ETFs trading above their 200-day average.
  • Liquidity: the US Treasury 10-year minus 2-year yield (via FRED) and the 6-month change in retail money-market fund assets (monthly, via FRED, shifted 35 days so history only uses what was already published). Money flowing into cash scores as risk-off.

Composite = average of the three axes. Regime flips to risk-on above +0.15 and to risk-off below −0.15; between them it keeps its last state, so it does not flicker.

Known weaknesses

  • Four of the six indicators are measured in the United States. The G20 enters through breadth and the country globe. Russia has no listed country fund since 2022 and the African Union has none, so both are missing from the globe.
  • Data updates every weekday after the US close through a GitHub Action, so a signal is at most one trading day old. Prices from Yahoo Finance can be revised or missing. An indicator whose source stops updating is dropped, not carried forward.

Mazor Studio · waves

Ride the wave, leave first

Which sectors, themes and commodities are moving now, and what the same in-and-out rule did on past events.

loading market data…

Information, not investment advice. A signal says a wave is visible in prices, not that it will continue.

Wave scanner

Which themes are moving now

Sector, theme and commodity funds, checked every weekday after the US close. IN: the fund beats the S&P 500 by 5% or more in 20 days and stays above its 100-day trend against the index for five days. OUT: the same exit as above. The scanner sees the wave in prices; the event behind it is named only where a source confirms it.

Same rule on the last five years

Theme trades

In when it is obvious, out on the break

One trade per event. IN: the day the event was obvious to everyone. OUT: the first week the theme stops beating the S&P 500 (five days below its 100-day trend against the index), filled at the next close. Each result sits next to what the S&P 500 did over the same days.

Method

How the waves are read

Strength of a fund = its price divided by the S&P 500 (SPY). A wave starts when that strength rises 5% or more in 20 trading days and stays above its own 100-day average for five days. It ends when the strength stays below that average for five days, after at least 20 days in. Every trade fills at the close of the day after the signal, so no result uses a price that was not yet known.

Known weaknesses

  • On five years of data the scanner rule did not beat the S&P 500 on average. It finds waves; it does not pick the ones that pay.
  • The 43 funds are US-listed and the yardstick is the S&P 500. Waves in other markets show only through global sector and commodity funds.
  • The event trades start on dates chosen after the fact, when the event already looked obvious. That is hindsight; twelve events are case studies, not proof.
  • Causes are named by hand and only with a source. A missing cause means no confirmed source yet, not that there is no cause.
  • No costs, taxes or currency effects are included.

Mazor Studio · delta neutral

No direction, just the gap

Two ways to earn without betting on up or down, each run on £1,000 of paper money. Nothing here is invested.

loading…

Paper money, not investment advice. UK retail clients cannot trade crypto perpetuals: the FCA bans crypto derivatives for retail, and Kraken offers them in the UK only to professional clients. In the EU, Kraken offers them to eligible retail clients. The pairs can be run in the UK through spread bets or CFDs.

Strategy 1 · crypto funding carry

Hold the coin, short its perpetual

£500 buys the coin, £500 is margin for a 1x short of the same amount. Price moves cancel. While traders pay to stay long, the short collects that funding every hour; when funding turns negative, the short pays.

Funding by month, % a year

Strategy 2 · stock pairs

Long the cheap twin, short the dear one

Seven pairs of similar companies. When the gap between them stretches to two standard deviations of its last 60 days, buy the cheap one and short the dear one, £500 each. Close when the gap is back under half a deviation, after 30 trading days, or if it stretches past four.

Method

What is assumed

  • Carry: funding from Kraken's public history for the BTC and ETH multi-collateral perpetuals, per hour. Fees at Kraken's lowest tier with limit orders: 0.40% spot and 0.02% perpetual, paid once in and once out. No price gap between coin and perpetual, no currency effect between pounds and dollars.
  • Pairs: daily closes from Yahoo Finance. Hedge ratio from the last 250 days, gap measured against its last 60 days, every trade filled at the close of the day after the signal. Costs: 0.1% dealing spread per leg each way and 5% a year overnight financing on one leg while a trade is open.

Known weaknesses

  • Delta neutral has to beat a savings account to be worth the risk. Compare the yearly figures with the cash rate you can get.
  • Carry earns only while longs are crowded. Funding turns negative in sell-offs, and the exchange itself is a risk: if it fails, both legs are gone.
  • A 1x short is liquidated only if the price doubles; more leverage earns more funding but can wipe the short in a spike.
  • Pairs assume two companies stay similar. When one changes course for good, the gap never closes; the stop at four deviations caps that loss but does not avoid it.
  • The seven pairs were chosen today, knowing which companies still exist and still look alike. That flatters the history.
